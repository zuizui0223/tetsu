# Contrasting functional associations of active railways, inactive railways and roads across global landscapes

Working manuscript — 2026-11-28; not submission-ready. Road-vector measurement test 46105819255 was running at preparation and is not included. The accompanying 50-row evidence ledger records selected exact estimates, approximate intervals, support and source job IDs, including non-estimable outcomes. It is not an exhaustive archive or meta-analysis.

## Abstract

Transport infrastructure can alter habitat and spatial connections, yet linear infrastructures are often treated as interchangeable ecological corridors. We assembled a global railway geometry and mapped-status database and combined it with species occurrence and trait data to assess whether associations with recorded community functional composition differed among active railways, inactive railways and roads. In 369 common 200-km cells across five represented continents, species-equal nonwoody plant stature had negative associations with active railway length but positive associations with inactive railway length and raster-implied road length in 2023–2024. Direct coefficient contrasts were negative for active versus road length (−0.03519, approximate 95% interval −0.05562 to −0.01475) and active versus inactive length (−0.01607, −0.02687 to −0.00528). The road contrast remained negative after family-centering, observer partitioning and individual represented-continent omission, but was uncertain in 2025. Bird community similarity exhibited an exploratory low- versus high-hand-wing-index association with mapped active railway crossings that was not supported after habitat and migration standardization. Butterfly edge affinity showed a conditional association with railway status, but the common three-trait analysis did not support a shared signature. These findings identify conditional differences among infrastructure types and traits rather than demonstrate a general railway-mediated dispersal or habitat-selection mechanism.

## Introduction — argument

Railways and roads share a linear spatial geometry, but their environments, traffic, vegetation and maintenance may differ. Corridors may influence communities through movement, establishment environments, or correlated landscape and recording processes. These are distinct hypotheses rather than interchangeable measurements. The comparison of mapped active and inactive railway infrastructure is useful because both retain a railway alignment; it is not a randomized closure experiment. Roads provide a further comparison. The question is whether functional-composition and cross-landscape similarity associations differ by infrastructure type and trait. Analyses are exploratory, with follow-ups frozen before their outcomes, and not a single prospectively registered study.

## Methods — analysis spine

Use Overture 2026-11-18 mapped geometry; conventional active candidates are standard/narrow/unknown-gauge segments without mapped inactive/construction flags, and inactive candidates are disused or abandoned flagged segments excluding construction. Census: 2,054,855 mapped rail segments, 2,237,043.481 km conventional active candidates and 487,283.453 km inactive candidates. The fixed iNaturalist snapshot is a source-wide photo-linked record census, not a survey of railway rights-of-way. Use species-level trait tables, species-equal summaries, explicit coverage and observer-disjoint diagnostics. The retained stature comparison uses common 200-km cells with ≥80% land and ≥50% trait coverage under original nonwoody denominator and support rules. Fit joint log1p km predictors for active rail, inactive rail and GRIP4 raster-implied road length, retaining prior environment, human footprint, effort, trait coverage and 1000-km block effects. Report direct differences with paired spatial scores and equal-year means. Actual vector-length comparison is a distinct source-measurement test, not independent biological data.

For birds use adjacent 200-km cells with rail-positive endpoints on fixed candidate geography, opposite observer halves, and low-minus-high HWI Jaccard similarity (cut 21). Compare binary mapped crossings, not traffic volume. Standardize within supported habitat-density × migration strata. Xeno-canto uses a fixed AVONET table but different exact-name intersection and normalized recordist names. For butterflies use species-equal edge affinity; for satellite cover use paired local mapped alignments and near-minus-background fractions. Keep separate units and supports; do not pool coefficients.

## Results

### Plant stature contrasts differ among infrastructure types

Earlier common-cell slopes were −0.00761 for active railway, +0.00846 for inactive railway and +0.02757 for road length. The direct active-minus-road difference was negative in 2023 (−0.04465 [−0.06920, −0.02009]) and 2024 (−0.02572 [−0.04424, −0.00720]), but uncertain in 2025 (−0.01991 [−0.04631, +0.00649]). We did not test a temporal trend. Family-centered and disjoint-observer comparisons yielded −0.03343 [−0.05171, −0.01515] and −0.03744 [−0.06459, −0.01028], respectively. All five represented-continent omissions retained negative intervals on primary support, but the less restrictive land50 sensitivity excluding Asia was uncertain. 328 of 369 cells were in North America or Europe; no South American block entered the primary sample. Earlier years reuse the same platform and selected geography, not independent biological validation.

The earlier fixed-total active share contrast for the full positive-rail sample was uncertain, and local overlap yielded zero eligible matched pairs on primary 200-km support. A local 80-m feasibility pilot found zero plant-height-supported pairs among 21 complete active/inactive queries. These limit identification rather than show absence of effects. ### Bird wing morphology and cross-landscape similarity

For 693 pairs in 2018–2022, the unstandardized low-minus-high HWI Jaccard difference was positively associated with active railway crossings after accounting for actual highway crossings (+0.03086 [0.00675, 0.05498]). The active-minus-inactive contrast was +0.03205 [0.00498, 0.05912], but active-minus-highway was uncertain (+0.02015 [−0.00523, 0.04553]). Habitat/migration standardization (396 pairs) yielded an uncertain active-minus-highway contrast (−0.00706 [−0.03591, 0.02179]); endpoint fixed effects changed the unstandardized direction. Directional new-record tests did not meet minimum support. The acoustic platform acquired 936,820 metadata records but yielded only 17 conditioned pairs (11 blocks) and no estimate. Coordinates lacked uncertainty metadata, and machine observations were included. This cannot confirm or refute the mechanism. ### Secondary habitat evidence and the joint signature

Butterfly edge-affinity active-minus-inactive slope was +0.01543 [0.00710, 0.02375] in 513 common 100-km cells for 2023–2024; the observer-half contrast was uncertain (+0.00816 [−0.00094, 0.01725]). In 123 paired satellite locations, near-minus-background contrasts in built-up and grass cover differed between mapped active and inactive rail (+0.08274 and −0.06400), but the primary tree contrast and vegetation composition decomposition were uncertain. Imagery (2021) predates mapped status (2026); historical status was not verified. The joint 530-cell test supported only stature in the hypothesized direction: edge affinity was uncertain and mean HWI had the opposite point direction. Mean HWI composition and guild-specific similarity are different outcomes. ## Discussion — claim boundary

The strongest claim is that conditional stature associations differ among mapped infrastructure types on a defined coarse landscape support. This does not establish shorter individual plants, wind tolerance, management adaptation or a physical equal-km substitution effect. Regional species pools, route geography, land use and detection remain competing explanations. Bird findings motivate a dispersal-compensation hypothesis but do not demonstrate that railways assist low-HWI birds. Habitat/migration conditioning and independent-source support failures preclude the stronger claim. Secondary insect and land-cover results inform context, not a causal pathway. Spatial plant–insect–bird co-diversity should be separate because railway specificity was not established. Earlier field-flora reanalyses are contextual data at different scales, not replications of the grid effect. Before submission include full study-selection history, complete model specifications, independent source feasibility and verified prior literature positioning. Consider a narrower stature paper rather than force an overarching cross-taxon mechanism if remaining tests do not support it.
